# Restricted share-token roadmap and ledger transition

Status: design proposal, 2026-10-26. No tokenized ownership or migration is implemented by this document.

Update, 2026-11-13: Stage 1 is implemented in contract schema v2 (deployed to testnet beside v1 on 2026-11-13): rounds, board-approved investor issuance paid atomically with the settlement token, and `reassign_holding` for wallet replacement. v2 deals also cover secondary sales and buybacks on the ledger, without a token. Captab's current ownership units and TESTUSD are fictional testnet assets, not legal shares.

## Decision

Keep the existing company ledger as the authority for the current MVP. Add investor issuance, round records, and wallet recovery as ledger features first. Tokenize only when a defined share class needs wallet-visible balances or controlled transfers. At that point, deploy one restricted NEP-141 token contract per genuinely fungible share class; its balances and supply become the **only** issued-ownership balance for that class. The company controller retains authorized capacity, grants, approval workflows, round records, and distribution orchestration. Grants are promises/reservations and do not become token balances until vesting and claim.

The current `Company` contract has `holders`, `issued_total`, `pool_available`, `grant_reserved`, grants, and distributions in one Borsh state structure. Its `issue_founder_units` and `claim_vested` mutate holdings directly; `prepare_distribution` snapshots those holdings. It has no transfer, investor, account-replacement, or upgrade method. The deployed testnet company still has its full-access deployment key; the documented final freeze has not been applied. See `contracts/company/src/lib.rs`, `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`, and `docs/RUNBOOK.md`.

## Stage 1: cover the early company journey without tokenization

1. **Investor round record.** Add a bounded `Round` keyed by a unique ID: `instrument_kind` (initially same-class direct issuance only), `opened_at`, `closed_at`, `currency`, `price_or_valuation_note_hash`, `document_hash`, `status`, and a list of issuance IDs. Keep investor names, subscription documents, bank details, and sensitive financial data off-chain. The hash is an audit reference, not evidence that funds arrived or legal rights exist.
2. **Explicit investor issuance.** Add DAO-only `issue_investor_units(action_id, expected_revision, round_id, recipient, units, consideration_ref_hash)`. It uses the same capacity/replay checks as founder issuance, records its own event, and requires an open/approved round. The company verifies off-chain settlement and documents before proposing the action. Do not use `issue_founder_units` for investors. No preferred rights, conversion, or automatic payment-versus-delivery are implied.
3. **Account replacement.** Add a DAO-approved, replay-safe `replace_account` for a holder who controls a new wallet. Require an explicit source/destination, evidence reference, notice period, recipient registration, and zero unresolved distribution entitlements or payment attempts for the source. Move the issued balance and active grant recipient in one contract call; preserve historical grants/events and the exact total supply. Frozen distribution recipients must not be silently redirected. If a holder has a pending historical payment, resolve it under a separate reviewed process before replacement.
4. **UI and audit.** Add round and investor issuance screens, an account-replacement review, and distinct event/history labels. Recheck all contract inputs and DAO policy at proposal time. Tests must cover duplicate round/issuance IDs, insufficient capacity, unauthorized caller, grant-account replacement, and blocked replacement during unpaid/in-flight distributions.

This stage supports first founders, a co-founder receiving immediate units, pool-backed employees, same-class direct investors, completed payouts, and reviewed wallet changes. It does **not** implement founder reverse vesting, options, SAFEs, preferred shares, investor payment verification, repurchases, or legal registration.

## Stage 2: token design, before any live migration

Use a `ShareToken` contract per class and a versioned `CompanyControllerV2`. Start with one class. The token stores `balances`, `total_supply`, a bounded holder index, storage registrations, transfer permits, and immutable balance snapshots. The controller stores the DAO and token ID, authorization/capacity, pool and grant reservations, rounds, action IDs, and payout state. It must never maintain a second mutable copy of issued balances. Read `ft_balance_of`/`ft_total_supply` from the token; any cached UI projection is non-authoritative.

Token mint and burn are limited to the controller through explicit commands with unique IDs. A claim reserves its amount in the controller, calls the token to mint, and finalizes only after a confirmed token result. Ambiguous cross-contract outcomes require querying the token's command ID before retrying. A failed callback must not create a second mint. The token checks the class's authorized ceiling or a controller-issued bounded mint allowance; do not rely on an off-chain cap calculation alone. The invariant is `token_total_supply + pool_available + grant_reserved <= authorized_units`, accounting for any pending mint reservation.

Transfer policy is deny-by-default. A narrow permit specifies source, destination, amount, expiry, purpose, and unique ID, and is consumed only by a matching holder-signed `ft_transfer`. No permit means no transfer. `ft_transfer_call` needs the same policy and a correctly implemented resolver if it is offered; it is not an unrestricted route around the guard. Model wallet recovery separately from a sale: where the old wallet cannot sign, a reviewed DAO recovery can burn/reissue or move an entire holding only under a documented authority and dispute procedure. Standard wallets may display the token, but generic exchanges will not be able to trade it. A transfer policy cannot prevent an off-chain sale of wallet control or contractual beneficial interests.

Implement and test NEP-141 core methods, NEP-148 metadata, NEP-145 storage behavior, and NEP-300 events. Use zero decimals if one unit equals one whole share, or define a fixed subdivision deliberately before launch. Changing decimals later changes how wallets display balances; it must not change the underlying integer ownership. Publish the contract/class ID, rights reference, transfer policy, and version in the app.

For distributions, the token must atomically capture `(snapshot_id, block height, ordered holder balances, total supply, hash)` in its own state at the record point. The controller obtains this frozen snapshot and derives entitlements. It may finalize from a previously recorded token snapshot if a callback fails. Reading token balances one holder at a time from the controller while transfers or mints continue is not a coherent snapshot. The existing fixed entitlement, DAO approval, exact TESTUSD funding, and per-recipient payment machinery can then be adapted to the snapshot. The class's payout rights remain a separate policy; one fungible balance is insufficient if classes later have different economics.

## Stage 3: rehearsal and cutover from the current ledger

**Recommended for this testnet project:** deploy fresh V2 controller and share-token accounts, rehearse a full import, and keep the V1 company as a read-only historical reference in the app. Do not mutate the existing rehearsed deployment or remove its key while the migration design is being tested. A production cutover needs a legally/operationally designated canonical contract and a way to retire V1 writes, not merely a UI switch.

1. **Finish obligations.** Settle every funded distribution and verify escrow is zero. Cancel or resolve any unfunded distribution under the existing rules. Investigate every in-flight or `NeedsReview` payment; do not infer failure from a timeout. Stop new proposals, claims, snapshots, and payouts, then DAO-pause V1.
2. **Export at a finalized boundary.** Record V1 network, contract ID, code hash, block height, ledger revision, authorized units, ordered holdings, supply, pool, all grants and their issued/capped amounts, distributions and payment statuses, and the DAO/policy. Hash a canonical export. Verify `sum(holdings) == issued_total` and `issued_total + pool_available + grant_reserved <= authorized_units`; reconcile grant reservations and completed payout totals. A paused V1 stops ordinary writes, but callbacks can still settle, so confirm no open attempts before export.
3. **Import once.** Initialize V2 and the token with the export hash and source identifiers. Mint exactly the exported issued units to the same accounts through a one-time, bounded bootstrap operation; import unissued grants and pool without minting them. Reject repeated or inconsistent imports and revoke the bootstrap capability afterward. No initial investor or employee should receive additional units as a side effect.
4. **Reconcile independently.** Compare every account balance, token supply, grant status, pool, authorized capacity, and the export hash. Exercise a new grant claim, investor issuance, blocked transfer, approved recovery, snapshot, and TESTUSD payout on a disposable Sandbox migration first, then a fresh testnet rehearsal. Test partial import interruption and restart before any live cutover.
5. **Designate the new authority.** Update the app's verified contract IDs/code hashes, DAO proposals, runbook, manifests, and any governing documents to name V2/token as canonical from a specific finalized block. Retain V1 and the export for audit. V1 is only truly retired on-chain if it is changed to archival code or otherwise made unable to write; its current DAO can later unpause it. If V1 is frozen before an archival upgrade, a social/UI declaration of V2 authority is possible, but the old contract remains callable and technically creates a competing ledger. Do not claim a trustless one-source cutover in that state.

**Alternate same-account route:** before deleting the V1 deployment key, deploy code with an explicit `OldState -> NewState` migration. This could expose NEP-141 views over the existing `holders` and `issued_total`, avoiding a balance copy for the first class. It is less aligned with a separate contract per class, changes the existing payout contract in place, and requires careful Borsh/state migration and regression testing. Simply deploying new WASM against the old state layout is unsafe. If the key is removed and there is no self-upgrade method, this route is closed for that account.

## Irreversibility and rollback windows

| Decision or action | Can it be undone? |
| --- | --- |
| Draft contracts, local/Sandbox tests, fresh testnet rehearsal | Yes; discard and redeploy. |
| V1 DAO pause | Yes; the DAO can resume under the current code. Pause alone does not make V1 archival. |
| Mint on-chain share tokens | The transaction history cannot be erased. If code and governing rules permit, balances can be corrected by a recorded burn/reissue or a later migration, but that is another auditable transaction, not a rollback of history. |
| Publicly designate V2/token as the ownership authority | Operationally difficult to reverse once holders and documents rely on it. A further versioned migration is possible, but must reconcile every holder and right again. |
| Remove the current company's last full-access key, with no self-upgrade method | Effectively irreversible **for that contract account's code**. A new account can be deployed, but the old contract cannot be modified to retire writes or add token methods. Do not run `pnpm freeze:company --apply` during this design work. |

No migration is authorized by this plan. Keep the current MVP release gates separate from the V2 project. Before using real equity or value, obtain jurisdiction-specific legal and transfer-agent advice about what record confers rights, registration, consent, recovery, taxes, and payouts.

## Suggested implementation order and acceptance gates

1. **Ledger extension:** round model and explicit investor issuance; account-replacement workflow. Pass Rust, Sandbox DAO, and app tests. No token dependency.
2. **V2 prototype:** restricted token + versioned controller on new accounts. Prove mint idempotency, transfer guards, storage, grant claim, recovery, and atomic token-side snapshot under concurrent actions.
3. **Migration harness:** finalized V1 export, reproducible hash, one-time import, independent reconciliation, interrupted-import recovery, and V1 retirement policy.
4. **Testnet cutover rehearsal:** use disposable accounts first; publish before/after balance table and receipt links. Keep the existing demo instance unchanged until a separate decision to migrate it.
5. **Production decision:** only after rights, upgrade/freeze policy, operator access, external audit, and legal source-of-truth are settled. Do not treat NEP-141 compliance as proof of legal share ownership or free transferability.
